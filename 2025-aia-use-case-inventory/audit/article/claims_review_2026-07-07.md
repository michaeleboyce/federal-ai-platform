## 1. Required phrasings (verification-driven)

1. **UK GDS trial**: "GDS centrally coordinated a cross-government M365
   Copilot trial ... with each organisation committing to at least 1,000
   licences." Do NOT call it a shared central platform or centralized
   procurement (2-1 vote; the dissent was exactly on that framing; UK
   procurement stayed departmental, trial ran in departmental tenants).
2. **All UK/Australia outcome numbers are self-reported** (UK 26 min/day
   from survey-range midpoints top-capped at 60; DWP 19 min/day SUR on
   ~48% response rate, opt-in cohort, evaluation itself warns of
   overestimation; Australia "up to 1 hr" self-assessed). Cite as
   "self-reported"; never as measured outcomes.
3. **METR RCT**: cite as a date-stamped early-2025 finding and a
   methodological warning (19% slower measured vs 20%-faster believed —
   the ~39-point perception gap), NOT as AI's current effect on
   developers; METR's Feb-2026 follow-up says tools have likely improved
   (weak evidence on magnitude).
4. **Singapore leaderboard**: "publishes a leaderboard of agencies with
   the highest AI use" — visibility (public vs internal-to-government)
   unspecified in the source; single-source, attested ~late 2024. Do not
   write "publicly publishes".
5. **Singapore 80%** is ever-used-among-150K-officers; the article's
   ~38% is evidenced-access-among-747K-eligible. Different metrics AND
   denominators — compare directionally, never in one chart axis.
6. **Accenture**: "rolling out to ~743K" = license provisioning in
   progress (April 2026), not verified active usage; the 89% MAU was
   measured on one ~200K tranche; all figures vendor/company-reported.
7. **Moderna**: "executive-set target with a deadline" (the source says
   "objective", not "mandate"); do not assert the target was hit; the
   120-conversations/user/week figure is OpenAI-marketing-only and
   unaudited — hedge or omit.

## 3. The self-report discount cuts against OUR OWN citations

The prior draft cites VA "2-3 hours per week saved, 70% report increased
efficiency" and CDC "41,000 hours / $3.7M / 527% ROI" favorably. These
are the SAME evidence class (self-reported surveys / program
self-assessment) as the UK/Australia numbers the article will treat
skeptically — and METR's perception gap applies to them equally. The
article must apply one standard: label every such figure self-reported,
or drop it. This is an honesty requirement, not a style choice.

## 4. The mandate already contains the training clause

The AI Action Plan text mandates "access to, **and appropriate training
for**, such tools." Singapore's strongest depth lever (mandatory AI
literacy course for all 150K officers, Oct 2025) is not a new idea to
import — it is the un-enforced half of the existing US mandate. The
CIO-certification plank should certify BOTH halves. (Verified mandate
text is quoted in the draft itself; Singapore course: MDDI PQ response
2025-11-06.)

## 5. New claims the article CAN now make (fact_sheet §8, each with
its verified phrasing and sources there)

- Fast provisioning was achieved elsewhere and still produced shallow
  use (Australia: licenses live ~6.5 weeks after announcement via
  contract piggybacking; only ~⅓ of participants used it daily).
- The depth cases paired access with mechanisms outside the article's
  current five planks: mandatory training (SG), funded champions/change
  management (Accenture, Moderna), visible usage telemetry (SG
  leaderboard; Accenture MAU), executive targets with deadlines
  (Moderna), self-service bot building (SG AIBots 20K+).
- No comparator government published coding-agent deployment data
  (pending absence-confirmation) — the US zero-live-agents measurement
  may be the first of its kind.
- The only telemetry-measured coding evidence is modest-positive
  (GitHub/Accenture RCT: +8.69% PRs, +15% merge rate, 2-1 vote,
  vendor-published, Management Science corroboration); frame the federal
  coding-agent gap as an evaluation-capacity gap, not assumed forgone
  productivity.
