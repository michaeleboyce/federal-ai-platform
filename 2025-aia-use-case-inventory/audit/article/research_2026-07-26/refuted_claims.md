# Refuted claims — DO NOT USE

Both claims below were killed by 3-vote adversarial verification (0-3).
Per the supersession rule, they are recorded here so they are never
reintroduced from memory or from the original source posts.

## Refuted 1 (vote 0-3)
**Claim:** The monitor maintains historical snapshots reachable via an 'IPv6 & DNSSEC SnapShots' page, with example archived data going back to at least February 26, 2012 — meaning a multi-year time series exists in principle, though the landing page itself does not state the full archival date range or display a ready-made government-wide percentage series.
**Source that suggested it:** https://usgv6-deploymon.nist.gov/
**Consequence:** a historical IPv6/DNSSEC time series canNOT be read off the NIST monitor's snapshot archive; it must be assembled from Wayback captures of the monitor (or its pre-2021 predecessor fedv6-deployment.antd.nist.gov).

## Refuted 2 (vote 0-3)
**Claim:** Roughly one year earlier (circa the October 2017 issuance of BOD 18-01), only about 20% of federal agency domains met the same compliance bar — providing a near-year-0 baseline datapoint and the '>30 percentage points' rise in the post's title.
**Source that suggested it:** https://www.proofpoint.com/us/blog/threat-protection/federal-spf-and-dmarc-adoption-more-30-percent-points-leading-bod-18-01
**Consequence:** the year-0 baseline for the strict (SPF + DMARC p=reject) metric is unknown; use Agari's 18% any-DMARC (Oct 2017) as the verified BOD 18-01 baseline and label the metric.
