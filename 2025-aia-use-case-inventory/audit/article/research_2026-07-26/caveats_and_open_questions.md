# Caveats and open questions — 2026-07-21 round

## Caveats

1) Coverage gap: only 3 of the ~16 candidates in the brief (DNSSEC, BOD 18-01 DMARC/HSTS, IPv6) produced claims that survived 3-vote verification — EFT/direct deposit (DCIA 1996), IRS e-file (RRA 1998 80% goal), phishing-resistant MFA (M-22-09), e-invoicing (M-15-19), GPEA, electronic records (M-19-21), EMV (EO 13681), Login.gov, data-center consolidation, and CAC precursors were NOT assessed in the surviving claim set; treat them as unresearched, not NOT CHARTABLE. 2) Two claims were refuted and must not be reused: the NIST monitor's snapshot archive reaching back to Feb 2012 (0-3 — so the IPv6/DNSSEC historical series from the monitor is unproven until assembled from Wayback), and Proofpoint's '~20% at Oct 2017' baseline for the strict SPF+p=reject metric (0-3 — use Agari's 18% any-DMARC baseline instead, and label the metric). 3) Metric heterogeneity in the DMARC series: Agari (any DMARC policy, n=1,106 self-selected monitored domains), Proofpoint (SPF + p=reject, n=1,311 official list), and GAO/NCATS (p=reject over mixed email-domains/web-hosts, 83 agencies) measure different bars over different denominators — a chart mixing them needs per-point methodology footnotes, and the Agari live URL is dead (cite Wayback captures). 4) DNSSEC series quirks: the FISMA-report percentage series only starts FY2010 (~year 2), the metric changes in FY2014 and vanishes in FY2015, the published figures are DHS scans not agency self-reports, and the denominator shrank mid-series due to OMB M-11-24 domain reduction. 5) Mandate-lifecycle footnotes: M-08-23 was rescinded by M-17-26 (2017), BOD 18-01 was replaced by BOD 19-02 (2019), and M-21-07 rescinded both prior IPv6 memos — none of these move the year-0 dates but all belong in chart source notes. 6) One DMARC claim passed only 2-1 (the Jan 2018 63% figure), though it has strong archival and CyberScoop corroboration. 7) Time-sensitivity: the NIST monitor snapshot and live CISA/whitehouse.gov URLs were verified as of 2026-07-21; vendor blog URLs are already rotting.

## Open questions

- Can a year-by-year IPv6 (and post-2012 DNSSEC) percentage series actually be assembled from Wayback captures of the NIST USGv6 monitor and its predecessor fedv6-deployment.antd.nist.gov? The monitor's own snapshot archive claim was refuted, so this assembly work is the gating step for the IPv6 curve.
- Do the unresearched brief candidates hold chartable series — especially EFT/direct deposit (Treasury Fiscal Service payment-volume data vs. the DCIA 1996 clock) and IRS e-file share vs. the RRA 1998 80% goal, both of which likely have long official annual series and were flagged in the brief as high-priority?
- Can the BOD 18-01 DMARC curve be extended past year 1.6 (May 2019) using CISA Cyber Exposure Scorecards, CyberScope data, or later Valimail/Agari federal reports, to fill years 2-8 of the 12-year window?
- For the DNSSEC series, can the FY2010-FY2013 FISMA percentages be reconciled onto one denominator with the LISA-paper zone counts and the NIST monitor's current ~90%+ signed rate, so the curve doesn't visibly jump when the measurement source changes?
