# Verification pass: Singapore's institutional model vs USAi (2026-07-07)

All URLs accessed 2026-07-07. Verified vs estimate/gap flagged. Full
source list at bottom.

## Headline

Singapore did not fund a product; it funded an INSTITUTION. GovTech
(~3,000 staff, 700+ in-house engineers, permanent-secretary-led, under
its own ministry MDDI) predates Pair by a decade. Pair and AIBots are
outputs of that institution, distributed FREE over pan-government
single sign-on. USAi is the inverse: a product with no comparable build
institution, on a slice of a cost-recoverable shared-services fund,
opt-in at 15 agencies, scheduled to flip to agency-paid in FY2027.

## Ownership correction (get this right or a Singaporean reader will catch it)

- **Pair Chat = Open Government Products (OGP)** — core team of **~8
  people** (pair.gov.sg/pages/about). OGP ≈ 200 people, "a startup
  within the government," inside GovTech.
- **AIBots = GovTech's Data Science & AI Division (DSAID) / LaunchPad**
  (runs on Azure OpenAI) — a DIFFERENT team. Never attribute both to OGP.

## Verified timeline (Pair)

Jan 2023 hackathon → Q2 2023 alpha (7 agencies) + beta (5,000 officers)
→ **Q3 2023 rollout to ALL public officers** (~8 months
hackathon-to-whole-of-government) → Nov 2024: 60K+ registered users,
10M+ messages → Nov 2025 (MDDI PQ): ~80% of 150,000 officers have used
Pair Chat. AIBots: July 2023 hackathon → Feb 2025: 40K users / 115
agencies / 12K bots → Nov 2025: 20K+ bots.

**⚠ "80%" = cumulative EVER-USED, not active.** Weekly-active figures
are far smaller and inconsistent across sources (tech.gov.sg shows
"4,500+ weekly active" on the Pair page, likely stale/single-product;
a late-2024 report-card figure ≈ 20K WAU for the suite). Frame as
"near-universal reach with a smaller committed-daily core"; never "80%
use Pair."

## Funding mechanism

Central appropriation → free at point of use, no sunset. Distribution
pre-solved by pan-government SSO (UK i.AI's own assessment: central
auth investment "made the rollout of new AI and data platforms to all
government departments rapid"). Talent: private-competitive pay,
performance bonuses "up to 50%" (UK i.AI on Singapore).

## Cost figures — the gaps and the traps

- NO published operating cost for Pair/AIBots (or any national gov LLM
  platform). ⚠ Do NOT use: Singapore's S$70M/US$52M National Multimodal
  LLM Programme (SEA-LION R&D, not Pair opex); UK Humphrey's
  "£20M/year" (projected SAVINGS, not cost); GovTech's stale 2017
  S$238.93M budget (all of digital government, not AI).
- **⚠ USAi is NOT "funded at $71M"** — the FY2027 FCSF direct
  appropriation request (~$71M) covers the whole portfolio (Login.gov,
  cloud.gov, USAi...); no USAi-specific line, spend, or FTE count is
  disclosed anywhere.
- Therefore: anchor the USAi contrast to the INSTITUTION and FUNDING
  MODEL, never to a dollar comparison.

## The documented contrast (safe to publish)

1. Institution vs product: GovTech (perm-sec-led, ~3,000/700+ eng; UK
   i.AI: "a very large team for any government, but particularly for a
   country with a population 10x smaller than the UK") + OGP (200) +
   DSAID — vs USAi, a platform inside GSA TTS with no disclosed
   engineering cadre.
2. Distribution: pan-government SSO, whole-of-service rollout Q3 2023 —
   vs opt-in agency-by-agency, 15 in, waitlist.
3. Funding direction: central-funded free with no sunset — vs free-now,
   agency-paid cost-recovery from FY2027 (FCSF statutorily must be
   cost-recoverable), a model that structurally taxes adoption.
