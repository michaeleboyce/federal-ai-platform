# Caveats and Open Questions

Deep-research pass 2026-07-06/07. Reproduced verbatim from `result.caveats` (split into its numbered points) and `result.openQuestions`.

## Caveats

(1) Self-report dominates: nearly every government outcome number (UK 26 min/day, DWP 19 min/day, Australia's up-to-1-hour savings, manager-perceived uplifts) is self-reported survey data, and each evaluation itself flags selection bias and possible overestimation; METR's perception-gap finding implies these figures may substantially overstate real productivity effects — the article should not cite comparator time savings as measured outcomes.

(2) Vendor sourcing: all Accenture and Moderna figures derive from Microsoft/OpenAI marketing case studies (Moderna's 120 conversations/user/week is unaudited anywhere); only the seat counts and MAU are corroborated by trade press, and all coverage traces to single announcements.

(3) Two findings rest on 2-1 votes: the UK GDS mechanism framing (use the softened 'centrally coordinated with per-organisation licence commitments' wording) and the GitHub/Accenture RCT figures (imprecise, vendor-published).

(4) Two claims were refuted and must not appear in the article: Singapore default-on browser provisioning of Pair (0-3) and the Accenture staged 2.5-year timeline (1-2).

(5) Coverage gaps vs. the original research brief: NO claims survived verification on US states/localities (Pennsylvania's ChatGPT Enterprise pilot, California, New Jersey, Utah, Texas), JPMorgan/Goldman/Morgan Stanley/Walmart/Klarna, Estonia's Bürokratt, Canada, Japan, or the Gulf states — the comparison table the author wanted is therefore incomplete on those rows and should not be filled from memory.

(6) Time-sensitivity (all URLs accessed 2026-07-06): METR explicitly says early-2026 tools are likely better than the early-2025 study period; Moderna figures date to April 2024 pre-restructuring; Singapore's leaderboard is attested only as of ~late 2024–Jan 2025 with unspecified internal-vs-public visibility; Accenture's rollout was in progress, not complete, as of April 2026.

## Open Questions

1. US states/localities produced zero surviving claims: what are the verified numbers from Pennsylvania's published ChatGPT Enterprise pilot evaluation (the most-cited state time-savings study), California's GenAI sandbox, and New Jersey's AI assistant + training program? These need a dedicated verification pass before the article cites them.

2. Has ANY government anywhere deployed coding agents/assistants to its developer workforce at scale with published usage or outcome data (e.g., UK i.AI, Singapore GovTech engineering, US DoD platforms)? Nothing surfaced, which itself may be the article's strongest comparative point — but absence of evidence needs confirmation.

3. What does Singapore's central product organization (Open Government Products / GovTech's Pair team) cost, how is it funded and staffed, and is it the right institutional analog for a fully-resourced USAi — i.e., what would the budget line actually look like?

4. Did the UK and Australian trials convert into enterprise-wide provisioning with sustained usage (DWP opened Copilot to all staff in April 2025; Australia's post-trial decision is unverified), and is there any post-rollout telemetry showing whether trial-era shallow usage persisted at scale?
