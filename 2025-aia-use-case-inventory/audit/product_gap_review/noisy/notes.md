## Headline finding

**All 217 gap rows across the three products are false positives generated by overly-greedy aliases.** None of the 217 gaps reviewed mention the canonical product as the deployed AI tool. There are zero `link` recommendations and zero `add_alias` recommendations.

This is the expected outcome for the "NOISY/GENERIC" slice — the audit charter flagged these three products as "the hardest cases" precisely because their aliases match unrelated text. The signal-to-noise ratio is effectively 0%.

## Per-product diagnosis

### NEC NeoFace (id=2763)

**Problem aliases:** bare `NEC` (3 chars).

The audit script `scripts/audit_undercovered_products.py` only enforces word-boundary regex when the alias is ≥4 characters. `NEC` slips through as a pure substring match, which fires on `connect`, `connection`, `connected`, `necessary`, `enclave`, `engineer`, etc. The other aliases (`NEC NeoFace`, `NEC (Nippon Electric Company)`, `NEC (algorithm only)`, plus `NeoFace` if it were registered) are well-formed but not currently doing the heavy lifting — the bare `NEC` alias generates ~100% of the noise.

**All 10 currently-linked use cases are correct DHS CBP/OBIM identity-verification deployments** (Mobile Fortify, Traveler Verification Service, PreCheck Touchless Identity, etc.) — every one carries `NEC`, `NEC (algorithm only)`, `NEC Algorithm`, or `NEC (Nippon Electric Company)` in the vendor field. Existing coverage is complete; no additional NeoFace deployments exist in the corpus that aren't already linked.

**Recommended fix:** drop the `NEC` alias entirely. Replace with: `NEC NeoFace`, `NEC (Nippon Electric Company)`, `NEC (algorithm only)`, `NEC Algorithm`, `NeoFace`. The standalone `NeoFace` token is safe — it's distinctive and doesn't collide with English. Going forward, populate should require either a co-occurrence of `NEC` with `NeoFace`/`Algorithm`/`Nippon` within ~50 chars, or a vendor-field exact match on `NEC*`.

### Microsoft Teams (id=2471)

**Problem aliases:** bare `Teams`.

`Teams` is a normal English collaborative noun. The free-text fields are dense with phrases like "security teams", "planning teams", "rulemaking teams", "project teams". 39 of 41 gaps fire only on this generic usage. The remaining two (id=41143 DOJ "AI Meeting Assistant" — "Integrates with Microsoft Teams"; id=43164 VA — "Implementing Office Hours using Microsoft Teams") *do* contain word-boundary "Microsoft Teams" but describe Teams as an integration target / meeting venue, not as the deployed AI capability. By the charter rubric, those are still false positives because Teams isn't the AI tool the use case deploys.

**Recommended fix:** drop bare `Teams`. Keep `Microsoft Teams`, `MS Teams`, `Microsoft Teams Premium`, add `Teams Premium` and `Copilot for Teams`. Even with the tightened set, the populate script should additionally require AI-tool framing (vendor=`Microsoft` AND a Teams-specific noun phrase like `Premium intelligent recap`, `Copilot in Teams`, `Teams Premium meeting summarization`). Otherwise generic "we use Teams to notify users" mentions will keep producing false links.

### Custom In-House AI (id=2532)

**Problem aliases:** `Custom`, `In-house`, `In-House`, `Developed in-house`.

`Custom` and `In-house` are general-purpose English adjectives that appear constantly: "custom reports", "custom training set", "custom mobile app", "custom HTML code", "in-house tooling". All 27 gaps are this kind of adjective noise. The longer phrase `Developed in-house` is fine (it only matched zero gaps in the regex check) but is redundant once we add the more idiomatic phrasings.

A subtlety: the `Custom In-House AI` canonical is itself a *catch-all category*, not a real product SKU. Many of the 27 gap use cases probably should be linked to it on substantive grounds (they describe agency-built AI), but the question is *which* ones — and that decision can't be made from substring matching on `custom`. The right approach is to populate this product **structurally** rather than by free-text search:

> A use case links to `Custom In-House AI` iff its vendor_name normalizes to `In-house` / `In-House` / `N/A` / `Agency staff` / `(Agency) staff and contractor teams` AND no other commercial product in the catalog matches against name/system/vendor.

That structural rule belongs in `populate_use_case_products.py` rather than in the alias list. The catalog aliases should only pick up explicit self-descriptions (`Custom In-House AI`, `developed in-house`, `built in-house`, `in-house developed`, `in-house built`) so that the alias matcher doesn't keep producing this kind of noise.

**Recommended fix:** drop `Custom`, `In-house`, `In-House`. Keep `Custom In-House AI`. Add `developed in-house`, `built in-house`, `in-house developed`, `in-house built`. Pair with a structural populate rule (vendor-field heuristic + commercial-product fallback) for the rest.

## Cross-cutting catalog recommendations

1. **Raise `WORD_BOUNDARY_MIN_LEN` from 4 to 5, and treat all aliases <5 chars as requiring a manual co-occurrence rule.** The 4-char threshold lets `NEC` slip through as substring (3 < 4 → bypass) and would let aliases like `Bard`, `Llama`, `Gemma`, `Vega`, `Onyx` produce similar noise if added later. Three-letter and four-letter acronym aliases should never be matched standalone.
2. **Forbid common English words as aliases globally.** `Teams`, `Custom`, `Connect`, `Copilot` (when not paired with a vendor), `Studio`, `Cloud`, `Hub` are all in the catalog or likely to be. Add a denylist (extend the existing `NOISE_ALIASES` set in the audit script) that blocks any alias whose lowercased form is in a stopword list (top-5k English words plus tech jargon) unless it co-occurs with a vendor token within 30 chars.
3. **Move catch-all canonicals to structural population.** `Custom In-House AI` (and likely future categories like "Generic Open Source LLM" or "Unspecified Vendor Tool") should not have free-text aliases at all — they should be assigned by `populate_use_case_products.py` based on structured vendor-field signals + the absence of a commercial product match.
4. **Add a populate-time co-occurrence check for vendor-coupled product names.** For products where the canonical name is `Vendor Product` (e.g., `NEC NeoFace`, `Palantir Foundry`, `Microsoft Teams`), require that both the vendor token and the product token appear within the same field or within ~80 characters of each other in the concatenated narrative. This eliminates the "Microsoft Teams" → "teams" and "NEC NeoFace" → "NEC" classes of error in one rule.

## What was NOT flagged as `unclear`

I considered flagging the two word-boundary "Microsoft Teams" hits (41143 DOJ AI Meeting Assistant; 43164 VA FAQ Gen AI) as `unclear`, but the charter rubric is explicit ("A real link requires the product to be the AI tool the use case deploys, not just a passing reference"). Both clearly use Teams as a UI/integration surface, not as the AI. They are `false_positive` under the charter.

No NEC NeoFace word-boundary matches existed in the gap set (all 10 real NEC mentions are already linked), so nothing to flag there. No Custom In-House word-boundary phrase matches (`Custom In-House AI`, `developed in-house`, etc.) appeared in gaps either.
